Universal Strategic Situation Analysis System

A guided analytical workflow for moving from situation definition to evidence-based strategy. Each stage defines what to collect, how to analyze it, how to interpret the result, and when to move forward.

  • Purpose - why the stage exists.
  • Required Inputs - what data, observations, or documents are needed.
  • Collection Methods - how to gather the information.
  • Analysis Logic - how to structure and evaluate the evidence.
  • Interpretation Guidance - how to understand what the evidence means.
  • Transition Rule - how the output feeds the next stage.

SWOT is treated as a synthesized strategic output, not a starting framework.

Step 1

Situation Definition

Purpose

Define the decision context, problem boundary, desired outcome, and analytical scope before collecting evidence.

Required Inputs

  • Problem statement, decision question, and success criteria.
  • Known symptoms, constraints, assumptions, and unresolved questions.
  • Time horizon, geography, organizational boundary, and affected users or units.

Collection Methods

  • Leadership interviews, intake workshops, document review, and initial observation.
  • 5W1H framing: what, who, where, when, why, and how.

Analysis Logic

Separate symptoms from the core decision, define what is inside and outside scope, and list hypotheses to test downstream.

Interpretation Guidance

A strong definition explains the situation clearly enough that different analysts would investigate the same problem.

Transition Rule

Move to external environment analysis once the question, scope, and decision criteria are explicit.

Step 2

External Environment Analysis

Purpose

Identify the external forces that shape the situation before mapping people, institutions, and markets.

Required Inputs

  • Regulatory, economic, social, technological, political, and environmental signals.
  • Industry trends, policy changes, funding conditions, geographic realities, and emerging risks.

Collection Methods

  • Desk research, market reports, policy scans, expert interviews, news review, and community signals.
  • Macro, meso, and micro context mapping.

Analysis Logic

Classify external forces by relevance, direction of impact, certainty, timing, and intensity.

Interpretation Guidance

Focus on forces that change incentives, constraints, demand, cost, legitimacy, adoption, or risk exposure.

Transition Rule

Move to stakeholder mapping once the major external pressures and context boundaries are visible.

Step 3

Stakeholder Mapping

Purpose

Identify all individuals, organizations, institutions, and groups that influence or are influenced by the system.

Required Inputs

  • Customer groups, users, beneficiaries, internal teams, suppliers, partners, regulators, investors, communities, and competitors.
  • Known formal authority, informal influence, incentives, dependencies, conflicts, and relationship links.

Collection Methods

  • Interviews, org charts, supply chain mapping, community research, partnership analysis, and field observation.
  • Relationship tracing: who funds, approves, blocks, adopts, delivers, regulates, or depends on whom.

Analysis Logic

Classify stakeholders by power, influence, dependency, risk/conflict, and relationship position. Map how stakeholders affect each other, not just how they affect the focal organization.

Interpretation Guidance

High-power stakeholders can authorize or block change. High-influence stakeholders shape beliefs and adoption. High-dependency stakeholders carry exposure if the system fails. Conflict risk highlights resistance, misaligned incentives, or governance gaps.

Transition Rule

Move to market / ecosystem mapping when the stakeholder network, power structure, dependency chain, blockers, and enablers are visible.

Step 4

Market / Ecosystem Mapping

Purpose

Map the broader system of actors, alternatives, channels, complements, substitutes, and institutional forces around the stakeholders.

Required Inputs

  • Competitors, substitutes, complements, customers, suppliers, partners, distribution channels, institutions, and platforms.
  • Market size indicators, adoption patterns, switching behavior, channel economics, and ecosystem bottlenecks.

Collection Methods

  • Competitor benchmarking, user interviews, app reviews, community analysis, desk research, and channel mapping.

Analysis Logic

Identify actor roles, dependencies, flows of value, points of control, competitive pressure, and ecosystem gaps.

Interpretation Guidance

Look for where power accumulates, where dependency creates fragility, and where unserved needs or structural openings appear.

Transition Rule

Move to business / system model analysis after the external actor landscape and ecosystem mechanics are mapped.

Step 5

Business / System Model

Purpose

Explain how the entity or system creates, delivers, captures, funds, governs, and scales value.

Required Inputs

  • Offer, customer segments, channels, revenue model, cost structure, partners, capabilities, incentives, and governance model.
  • Evidence from stakeholder and ecosystem mapping about adoption, trust, access, and constraints.

Collection Methods

  • Business model canvas review, operating documents, financial review, interviews, customer journey analysis, and process walkthroughs.

Analysis Logic

Test whether value creation, delivery, capture, and governance reinforce each other or work against each other.

Interpretation Guidance

A model is weak when users value the offer but incentives, channels, costs, trust, or governance prevent repeatable delivery.

Transition Rule

Move to operational value flow once the model logic is clear enough to trace how work and value actually move.

Step 6

Operational Value Flow

Purpose

Map where value is created, transferred, delayed, lost, duplicated, or captured in the actual operating workflow.

Required Inputs

  • Process stages, handoffs, actors, inputs, outputs, cycle times, errors, rework, and bottlenecks.

Collection Methods

  • Workflow observation, service blueprinting, process mining, interviews, ticket review, and time-motion analysis.

Analysis Logic

Trace each stage from trigger to outcome and mark where value rises, falls, waits, leaks, or becomes expensive.

Interpretation Guidance

Operational issues often reveal why an otherwise logical strategy fails in practice.

Transition Rule

Move to metrics and unit economics when the workflow is concrete enough to measure cost, speed, quality, and return.

Step 7

Metrics & Unit Economics

Purpose

Convert the mapped system into measurable performance, cost, value, risk, and sustainability indicators.

Required Inputs

  • Volumes, conversion rates, retention, revenue, contribution margin, cost drivers, cycle time, quality, risk events, and capacity.

Collection Methods

  • Financial data, analytics exports, CRM or ERP data, operational logs, surveys, and manual sampling.

Analysis Logic

Connect metrics to the business/system model and value flow, then identify which numbers explain performance rather than merely describe it.

Interpretation Guidance

Metrics matter most when they reveal leverage: which driver changes outcomes, margins, reliability, adoption, or strategic viability.

Transition Rule

Move to strategic diagnosis once evidence, model logic, operational flow, and performance economics can be compared.

Step 8

Strategic Diagnosis

Purpose

Synthesize all prior evidence into the core diagnosis: what is really happening, why it matters, and where intervention has leverage.

Required Inputs

  • Situation definition, external forces, stakeholder map, ecosystem map, model logic, value flow, and metrics.
  • Contradictions, bottlenecks, structural advantages, constraints, risks, and unresolved uncertainties.

Collection Methods

  • Synthesis workshop, root-cause analysis, constraint diagnosis, decision matrix, scenario analysis, and expert review.

Analysis Logic

Distinguish symptoms from structural causes, rank constraints by severity and fixability, and identify strategic choices.

Interpretation Guidance

A good diagnosis names the few issues that explain many observations and clarifies what should change first.

Transition Rule

Move to SWOT + action strategy once the diagnosis can support concrete strategic choices and prioritized interventions.

SWOT + Action Strategy

Purpose

Convert the strategic diagnosis into a concise decision narrative, SWOT synthesis, action plan, owners, and monitoring logic.

Required Inputs

  • Validated evidence from all eight stages, prioritized constraints, strategic options, and decision criteria.

Collection Methods

  • Synthesis review, leadership alignment, action planning, KPI selection, and owner assignment.

Analysis Logic

Translate internal evidence into strengths and weaknesses, external evidence into opportunities and threats, then convert priorities into action.

Interpretation Guidance

SWOT is useful only when each quadrant is backed by evidence and leads to a decision, intervention, or monitoring metric.

Transition Rule

Finalize when each action has an owner, expected behavior change, success metric, and review cadence.
